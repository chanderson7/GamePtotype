# Bannerfall

*Working title.* Real-time squad battles sized for a phone, inside a sandbox campaign in the spirit of Mount & Blade, set in an invented world. Built with **Godot 4.7** in GDScript, targeting iPhone first, with Steam and Android from the same project.

This slice is the start of a campaign: pick a people, ride the map of the Glass Sea, and fight the bandits on its roads in real-time battles, or send your men in without you. Your warband carries over between battles and has to be paid and fed: the men you lose stay lost until you pay to reinforce them in a town, bandit bounties and spoils keep the purse full, squads that kill can be trained, and renown lets you lead more squads.

## What's in it

### The campaign

- **Lords and bandits you can read on the map.** Each realm's lords ride their own horse (a bay for Vardmark, a sand horse for Kharzan, a near-black one for Ryumon) under the realm's cloth and banner, with a knight's great helm and lance, an emir's turban and curved blade, or a Ryumon lord's crested helmet and back-flag; their men march behind, one per 45 men up to two, so a host's size is counted, not guessed. Bandits look like their region's outlaws (hooded brigands, masked desert reavers, ronin in straw hats, Wako pirates), sit round a fire while they lie in wait, and carry torches under smoke while they raid. Name plates are edged red for enemies, brass for friends, and carry a chip with the men and how they compare with yours (▲ stronger, ◆ about even, ▼ weaker).
- **Realm banners.** Each realm flies its own banner, with its own shape so you can tell them apart at a glance: Vardmark a pointed gonfalon with a silver keep on blue, Kharzan a swallowtail with a crimson sun over teal waters on saffron, Ryumon a tall nobori with three crossed arrows on red; your own Free Crown a gold crown on night, and bandits a torn rag with crossed knives. They fly over castles and towns and with parties on the map, in settlement views, over a besieged keep and on the siege result, on Home and on the new-campaign cards.
- **A Home screen for your company.** Your company on one card over the drifting map: the camp with your banner and title, where and when you left off, men, silver, food and renown as tiles, your captains' faces, and *Waiting for you* (unread letters, your contract, the wounded, the next step), each a tap from where it leads, then Continue. New campaign (with an explicit replacement confirmation), Learn to play and Settings sit in a small row below. The first time, Home shows the three peoples to begin with. Settings has its own page, reachable from Home or the map's Menu; Warband stays focused on your company.
- **Optional tutorial.** Opt in when choosing your people, or replay the six-step introduction from Home or Settings. Go back, skip, or leave at any point without advancing the campaign.
- **Resources and warnings.** Silver, food and renown have labeled icons in the map's top-right corner. Tap one for details; food is shown in days. Low supplies warn that men may leave. If they do, a saved warning names the losses and causes, explains what to fix, and pauses time until acknowledged.
- **Your first bond.** When a surviving captain pair first reaches rank C, a one-time introduction explains morale recovery, nearby deployment, and the later B and A bonuses. Open Warband directly to review the pair; the acknowledgment is saved with the campaign. After that, bonds never stop you: a battle's results tell them once (ranks reached first, then "Fenwick remembers Brann" for bonds a death ended, then one line, "7 other pairs grew closer", that opens the rest), and each new rank's talk waits at the fire.

- **The Glass Sea.** An invented inland sea shared by three realms: the **Kingdom of Vardmark** on the northwest shore (stone keeps and armored knights), the **Sultanate of Kharzan** across the southern deserts (walled cities and swift riders), and the **Ryumon Shogunate** on the islands beyond the eastern strait (disciplined archers and spearmen). Twenty-one settlements: in each realm a town, two castles and four villages, every village bound to a castle or town, joined by roads and three sea lanes. Who holds each place, and who is at war with whom, are the world's own and kept in your save, ready for the realms to fight over them.
- **Choose your people.** A new campaign starts with a choice of culture. Your captains take its names, your companies its words ("Brann's Pikes", "Tariq's Spears", "Hideo's Yari"), your troops its names (Footmen, Askari, Samurai), and you start at its capital. Cultures fight alike for now; their stats will differ later.
- **Ride the roads.** Tap a settlement to see it and ride there. Time passes only while you ride or wait, an hour a second (two at 2x), and stops the moment anything needs you. Sea lanes are slower than roads. Drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom, double-tap to find your party.
- **A living map.** A ragged coastline with beaches and surf breathing against it, turquoise shallows over a deep sea, hills shaded by the light, and each realm's own country: Vardmark's meadows, woods and grey peaks, Kharzan's sand, dunes, palms and red mesas, the Ryumon isles' pines, cherry blossom and snow-capped mountains, with fields, dry plots or paddies around every village. Three rivers run down to the sea: the Raven past Ravensford, the Qamar through the desert, and the Kumo on the isles, each forded where a road crosses it. Each culture builds its own towns, castles and villages: Vardish stone keeps under slate and red tile, Kharzani sandstone walls, domes and minarets, Ryumon castles of stacked black roofs on stone bases. Your rider trots along the road under your banner, kicking up dust; bandit bands march under ragged ones. The light follows the clock, gold at dawn and dusk and blue at night, when the settlements' lamps are lit, and cloud shadows drift across land and sea. A campaign starts at eight in the morning.
- **Lords on the map.** Each realm has its ruler and four lords, two of them holding its castles, riding the roads with warbands of five to nine squads under captains of their people, more of them trained as their renown grows. Each has a bearing that steers him (*Daring*, *Cautious*, *Honorable*, *Cruel*). They keep watch on their lands, drive off bandits caught raiding their villages, ride to defend a village the enemy is burning, raid the villages of realms they're at war with (a village left burning pays nothing and offers no recruits for a week), and go home to refill when their warbands are spent. Lords of realms at war who meet fight it out, settled as a fight you send your men into would be, and the loser regroups at his seat. A realm's treasury takes in what its places pay and pays its lords. Tap a lord to see who they are (with a portrait and their house's banner), how strong, and what they're about; zoom in to see their names. Lords at war with you stop you on the road; friendly ones stop to talk while you ride. Each speaks in the manner of their bearing, and one who has fought you before remembers it.
- **Talking to lords.** Meet a lord and you talk face to face, where you met: before a painted place (Ravensford, Qasr Hamra, Kitayama, your camp) or the settlement as the game paints it, at the hour it is, with their portrait, their banner and yours. One exchange at a time: they speak, and you answer with a line tagged with what it does, nothing irreversible on a stray tap. Friendly lords stop you when they have something to say; tap one nearby (or a lord of a realm at peace with you) to *Talk*, and a lord blocking your road can be talked to before steel is drawn.
  - *Regard*, -100 to 100, is what a lord thinks of you: what you've done to and for them, on top of their realm's opinion and your fame. It shows on their map sheet, in the talk and in People.
  - *News*: what's happened in the war since a lord last told you, and what they think of their own ruler.
  - *Stories*: tell one from your chronicle or the company's tales, each to each lord once. Cautious lords like prudence, Daring ones boldness, Honorable ones modesty and Cruel ones a hard hand; the game remembers what you learn.
  - *Troubles*: brigands on their lands become a job in your journal (haggle for silver, if they don't mind haggling). Done, it's worth +15 Regard, the biggest single source.
  - *Winning a lord over*, once you've raised your own banner: their conviction fills toward 100 from their Regard, jobs done for them, their ruler's failings once they've told you of them, arguments they like and silver. At 100 they swear on the spot, and a landed lord brings their castle and its villages. Push arguments they dislike and they refuse you (-10 Regard and a week's wait). A lord won mostly with silver is *bought*, and goes back, castle and all, if they come to think badly of you. Rulers never change sides.
  - *Steel*: challenge a lord to battle (at peace, that means war) with the stakes shown first, or to single combat with one of your captains as champion (no armies, no war: win for Regard and renown, lose and your champion is wounded for two battles). A Cruel lord blocking your road takes tribute.
  - *Captives* are talked to at your camp: swear them to your crown (Honorable ones won't), release them for honor (+25 Regard, +10 with their realm), ransom them, or keep them in chains, which Honorable lords everywhere hear of.
  - *People → Lords* lists every lord you've met: their Regard, what you know of them, whether they'd swear, and a pin to find them on the map.
- **Bandits.** Two bands roam the villages of each realm. When one blocks your road you can lead the fight, in a full battle against an army led by captains of that realm; send your men in without you, settled on the spot at the cost of a few more men; or withdraw to the last settlement you passed. Your scouts size up the fight first: who's stronger, and about how many men you'd lose sending them in. A beaten band is gone; another rises in its realm a day later. If they beat you, your survivors regroup at your capital half a day later, and the band keeps only the men it had left.
- **Garrisons.** Castles and towns keep guards of their local culture: up to 80 men in a castle, with more trained troops, and 120 in a town. They start half staffed and recruit a few men each day from their owner's treasury after the lords are paid, while keeping a reserve for wages. Settlement descriptions show the current guards and capacity; losses and empty garrisons persist in your save.
- **Romance and allies.** Meet three women attached to noble houses: Edda, the Vardish steward at Ravensford; Farah, the Kharzani envoy at Qasr Hamra; and Kaede, the Ryumon archivist at Kitayama. Each has a distinct five-chapter route and six optional illustrated personal conversations, with honest and strained branches, repair restitution, public weddings, private vows, or sworn friendships. Form house pacts for mutual protection, emergency household grants (up to 150 silver from a solvent lord), and replacement sponsorship escrow to fund allied recruits. In People & Letters each person has a card (portrait matching their mood, status, trust, the five chapters of their story, the next step and its distance, and one button to ride there or speak with them) and a page of their own (their story chapter by chapter, what they care about and won't stand for, dated memories). Their letters are real letters: written for them and for whether you're courting or friends, on parchment with the place and day, a signature and their house seal. Answer each with a short reply you read before it goes (a warm one warms them a little). New letters arrive as a banner on the map that never stops the clock, and the menu button counts what's unread. Toggle romance invitations in Settings.
- **Talking with them.** Speak with someone and you're face to face in their room (Edda's common room, Farah's envoy's chamber, Kaede's archive), at the hour it is, their painted portrait changing with the moment, their status and trust on the nameplate (the bar is marked at 60, where vows and a wedding open). One place holds everything: *Our story* (the chapter's decisions, gold, each saying what it does: +15 trust, −10 house standing, 100 silver, now courting; vows, a wedding and parting ask once more), time together (a few visits at a time, read as a conversation: their lines on the left, yours on the right, narration between; tap to go on; "No wrong answers", no numbers, a short version if you like), their unread letter, and *Remember when…*, where you bring up a memory and they answer with what they remember of it. A kept visit ends with a keepsake: "A memory kept…", and "+1 trust (2 of 3)" only when trust moved.
- **The wedding.** Ask before her house and she says yes, then asks where: her house's seat, your own seat if you hold one, or another friendly town near you (100 silver and 100 rations are set aside, and come back if you call it off). The place is made ready by the first dusk a day later, she writes when it is, and a gold ring marks it on the map; it waits for you, with no deadline. A siege, ashes or a realm at war with you holding the place postpones it, and her letter asks you to choose again. Ride there and *Begin your wedding*: the place is dressed in pixel bunting, both banners and lanterns; *Who came* shows her house's head as host, lords who think well of you, your sworn lords if you wear a crown and all your captains, and says who stayed away and why. Her vow comes from a time you shared ("She remembers: the spare spoon"); yours is one of three, none wrong. Then her people's rite (the Vardish cord, Kharzani bread and salt, Ryumon's three cups), a toast from your longest-serving captain and one from the host, and you raise your cup (to her, to the company, or to absent friends, by name, if captains have fallen). One closing card shows the kept memory with your words, the marriage pact in plain words (it protects her seat and yours), Regard from the lords who came (+10 host, +5 each other), and +15 renown. Private vows happen right in her room by one candle, with the same vows, no guests and no cost, and say plainly that her house wasn't asked. Her page then reads "Married · since day N", and the wedding in her memory book tells itself again with your own vow and toast.
- **The hall and gatherings.** Every town and castle has a Court tab ("Court · 3") listing who is really there: the lord who holds it if they're home, lords resting on their way, the household. Each row has their face, what they're doing ("Holds Ravensford · at home", "Visiting · resting before the road"), their Regard, and a brass "would like a word" when they have a trouble or a visit is open. *Away* says where the holder has gone, with *Show on map*. Tap a lord to talk indoors, in their culture's hall drawn in pixel art (a Vardish great hall with its dais and hearth, a Kharzani courtyard of arches and lanterns, a Ryumon audience hall of paper screens and tatami), tinted for the hour, with the holder's banners. No swords under a host's roof: a challenge becomes "Meet me outside the walls at dawn", a duel. In peace each realm holds a gathering about every two weeks, and a peace treaty brings a peace feast at the capital (and taking a castle, a victory feast there once it's safe): the ruler at the capital or a rich lord at their seat, three nights from the next dusk, a feast or a dance by its culture's name (feast or midsummer dance, banquet or lantern night, moon-viewing or blossom festival). Up to eight lords within two days' ride come, unless they're on campaign, raiding, under orders or refilling; their plates read "riding to the peace feast", and the hall glows with a ring of lanterns on the map. You're invited if you serve the realm, hold land in it, rule an ally, or the host's Regard is 10 or more: it comes as a banner like a letter, and sits in the journal with *Who's coming* and *Ride there*. During it the tab is *Feast · 6* in brass: your first word with each lord at the table is +2 Regard (+1 uninvited), and you can raise a toast once (renown 120: +2 with everyone there), bring the host a gift (50 silver or rations: host +5, realm +2), and hear the gossip once a night (an hour; it only says true things: a lord sour with their ruler because their castle burned or was lost, a lord's trouble, the latest war news). A dance night (every night of a dance, the last of a feast) offers one partner: someone you court (time together, +1 trust up to three dances, kept as a memory), a lord (+3 Regard, +4 if Daring), or the wall and its gossip. War, a siege or an enemy army near breaks it up, and the news says why. With a crown, your seat's Fief tab holds your own (feast or dance, 300 silver and 200 rations, once every 20 days, at peace): sworn lords always come, and you choose whether to ask friends, lords you'd win over, and their lieges (leave a liege out and it's a small snub); each guest is +5 Regard once, and lords you're persuading arrive 10 points more convinced. Between gatherings, lords near their capital sometimes rest there a while, so halls are rarely empty.
- **Memories you can open.** Each person's page keeps a memory book: every visit, decision, letter, ceremony, dance and move, with its day, place and an icon. A visit tells itself again (as a memory: it changes nothing), a letter opens, a decision shows what you said and how they answered, a move shows the place on the map. A settlement's Court says "Edda would like a word · new" when there's something new; nothing starts on its own.
- **Silver, food and wages.** You start with 400 silver and five days of food. Every day costs each man a ration and his wages (more for archers, more again for horse, half again for each level of training). Men who go unpaid or hungry desert, about one in ten a day, fewer if you're only a little short, and the map tells you when it happens. The top bar shows your silver, how many days of food you have and your renown, in red when you're running short.
- **Spoils and renown.** Beating a band pays a bounty plus silver and food for every man cut down, and wins renown. You can lead six squads at first, and one more for every 60 renown, up to twelve.
- **Culture troop trees and training.** Training a squad to level 2 in any town presents a two-card choice between two distinct branches for that troop type and culture (e.g., Vardish Men-at-arms vs Sergeants, Knights vs Mounted Sergeants; Kharzani Javelin Askari vs Guard Askari, Horse Archers vs Faris Lancers; Ryumon Hatamoto vs Ronin, Naginata vs Yari Ashigaru). Level 3 strengthens the chosen branch. Six specialized mechanics: armor and piercing, pre-contact javelins, horse archer skirmishing and saddle-shooting, melee sweeps hitting secondary foes, and steady morale.
- **Varied encounter objectives.** Not every battle is an open field rout:
  - *Slay the Bandit Chief*: Led by a named chieftain at the head of a tier-2 bodyguard squad, which waits behind his line until the fight turns. Felling or routing the bodyguard drops the entire host's morale by 35. Break the guard while your men are on it and the chief goes down in the rout, with no one to drag him clear, and the death roll decides his fate: slain, for double bounty and +10 renown, or carried off by his men after all. A chief who gets away comes back five days later at the head of a new band.
  - *Ambush*: Caught on a wooded road, your warband is strung out in a column three abreast, unbraced, with the enemy coming at both ends and no time to form up. You cannot withdraw. Strike back at once, before they close in.
  - *Village Defense*: Arriving at a village being raided by bandits triggers a timed defense: the village lies behind your line, and you hold its square for 2 minutes while more raiders come at it in waves from east and west. If they hold the square two to one for 25 seconds in all, they fire the village and the fight is lost; a ring of flame round the square shows how near they are.
- **Battlefield terrain.** The ground comes from where the fight happens on the world map: hills in high country, woods where the map is wooded, and a river with its fords wherever the fight is near one of the map's rivers:
  - *Hills & Ridges*: Vantage grants archers up to +25% range. Blows struck down a slope land up to 40% harder, and up one as much softer; uphill charges yield no bonus.
  - *Woods & Forests*: Slows foot by 25% and horse by 50%. Treetops deflect 50% of incoming arrows, halve volley shock, and prevent charges; blows struck into the trees from outside land 20% softer. Each stand of trees is drawn on its own dark forest floor.
  - *River Fords*: Impassable deep water forces armies through shallow fords where troops are slowed, take 25% more damage, deal 20% less, and cannot brace.
  - *Rocky Passes*: Natural bottlenecks with rock formations that block movement while letting arrows arc over.
  - *Village Streets*: Buildings and walls channel combat into tight streets and defense perimeters.
- **Chosen ground.** A band met on hills or in woods has sometimes picked its ground first (three times in ten when it isn't raiding or ambushing): it waits for you on the brow of a ridge or at the edge of a wood, and your scouts tell you so. Attacking it there costs you; going round or drawing it off is up to you.
- **Settlements.** Enter any settlement to buy food (cheapest in villages) and hire the recruits looking for work: a full squad of fresh men of that place's own culture under a captain of their own, so a Vardish warband can field Ryumon archers. Villages raise foot and bows, castles spears and horse, towns anything; recruits taken come back three days later. In a town you can also refill your squads (fresh men water down a squad's experience) and train squads that have earned it to the next level, which makes their men tougher and deadlier and raises their wages. Wait for a few hours anywhere.
- **Places remember what happened.** Settlement vistas and map markers reflect current ownership, friendly or hostile banners, local services, garrison pressure, active raids, damage, rebuilding and night lighting. Towns stage markets and smiths, castles guards, and villages field work. The same saved facts produce the condition sentence, recent-event note and Vardish, Kharzani or Ryumon local ambience.
- **War and politics.** Realms track war weariness from casualties, raids, and losses. Exhausted realms seek peace treaties with 30-day truces and daily silver tributes. The Ryumon Shogunate maintains the mainland balance of power, intervening if either rival holds 4 or more seats. View active wars, truces, and realm standing on the War & Politics sheet.
- **Mercenary service.** Offer your sword to realms at war for a 30-day mercenary contract with daily pay and lord capture bounties. Fighting under contract aligns your diplomacy with your employer; hostile realms bar you from their markets.
- **Armies, sieges, and conquest.** Marshals call expeditions of allied lords to march on enemy seats. Sieges construct rams and ladders, starve out defenders, resolve relief battles, and storm breached walls. Captured fortresses pass to the victor with all bound villages.
- **Allied battles and reserves.** Fights beyond warband scale feature a field cap of 12 active squads per side, with queued reserves marching in from the rear edge as frontline units break. Allied lord contingents fight under their own AI commanders with cyan trim and 1:1 casualty conservation.
- **Prisoners, ransom, and grudges.** Defeated enemy lords can be taken prisoner in your baggage. Settle ransom with envoys after 48 hours, deliver them to your employer, or release them for honor. Enemy lords who slay your captains hold a grudge, hunt you on the road with custom dialogue, and fight with heightened morale until avenged.
- **The Chronicle and map readability.** Review the last 30 world and company events categorized by wars, sieges, battles, and deeds. Map hosts scale by troop strength, marshal armies group cleanly, bandits fade at wide zoom, fluttering heraldic banners indicate owner realms, and siege camps and burning ruins are drawn around settlements.
- **The Journal.** The map's Menu opens a journal of everything the company has promised: jobs, a contract, captive lords, your wedding (choosing a place, being made ready, ready, or postponed), gatherings you're invited to, your fief. Each shows its objective, reward, cost, time left and why it can't be done yet; *Show on map* finds it (and follows a hunted band as it moves), and one can be pinned to a card under the top bar. Recently ended promises say how they ended.
- **Playable sieges.** Under contract or holding a fief, ride to an enemy castle or town and *Storm the walls*: lead the assault in a battle with a wall across the field and a gate your men on foot must batter down (the garrison holds behind it, sheltered from arrows, until it gives), or send them in. Friendly lords camped at the walls join you. A win hands the place to your side. Riding with an allied army, you're asked to lead its assault; when an army storms your own fief while you're there, you can hold its walls yourself, with the garrison beside you. Besieged places are ringed on the map with the besiegers' camp, and their sheet shows both sides, the walls, the food left and when the assault comes.
- **Your own crown.** A famous company holding a castle or a town (not a village: a crown needs walls) can raise its own banner at its seat: the seat becomes the capital of a new realm in your colours, the old liege goes to war, and every castle or town you storm after that is yours, paying your purse and raising its garrison from the crown treasury. Its own screen (Menu → Your Crown) has four tabs:
  - *Realm*: name your crown and pick its colour (banners and the map follow), see the treasury and what each day brings in, draw from the treasury (your lords notice) or pay into it, and track the goal: hold Highcliff, Khalun and Kurogane and the Glass Sea is yours (+100 renown; play on as long as you like). Hold nothing for three days and the crown falls: its lords go back to their old allegiances, its wars end, and the company rides free to raise a banner again another day.
  - *Holdings*: every castle, town and village with its garrison, who holds it and a pin to find it. Grant a castle or town to a sworn lord (+15 Regard; whoever held it before resents losing it) and it becomes their seat; take it back at a cost. At a crown castle or town that isn't your seat, its Crown tab does the same and lets you station your own squads with the garrison.
  - *Lords*: your sworn lords, their Regard, land and what they're doing. Order one to ride with you (they follow you and join your fights), to hold a place, or to go about the realm's business. The same orders are lines in a conversation with them.
  - *Diplomacy*: a call to arms (two ready lords gather at your seat and march on the enemy castle or town you name; ride with them from their marshal's sheet), and each realm: at war, at peace, allied or under truce, how weary of war they are, and what they pay or are paid. Declare war, propose an alliance, or sue for peace, whose terms show first: the wearier side pays the other tribute for a while. A crown worth fearing draws rivals: a rested realm stronger than you may declare war on it.
  - Captive lords can swear fealty (honorable ones won't), and lords on the road can be won over in conversation.
- **Company deeds.** Ten optional deeds, from serving a contract to its end to storming walls or keeping a captain alive through ten battles. The Warband shows your progress; each earned deed is a name the company can carry ("Known as the Wallbreakers"). Names bring nothing else, and no deed ends the campaign.
- **Camp feels like home.** The Warband's camp shows every captain at the fire, ready or resting with wounds, each a tap from their profile, under the light of the hour. One optional moment at a time comes from what really happened: a captain mourned by the friend they were bonded to, the captain who fought hardest speaking of the last battle and where it was fought, a shortage, a wound.
- **Consequences that linger.** Notable battles are recorded where they happened, and for three days the map marks them with crossed blades, and changes of hands with a fresh banner, before they fade into the chronicle.
- **Saved as you go**, on the device (`user://campaign.json`): after every arrival, encounter and battle, and every few seconds on the road. Continue from the title screen.
- **Saves of your own and going back**: up to 8 saves from the campaign menu, plus two the game keeps for you (just before your last battle, and when you last entered a settlement), in `user://saves/`. Revert, or tap any save, to see what going back would undo before it loads. An **Ironman** campaign (chosen at the start) has only the autosave.

### The battles

- **Four troop types with a counter loop.** Spearmen stop cavalry, cavalry ride down archers, archers shred infantry, infantry cut through spear lines.
- **Squads of live soldiers.** Twenty men to a squad (sixteen horse), three ranks deep. Each man fights on his own, keeps formation on the march, and breaks ranks when the lines meet. Cavalry hit hard on the charge, except into spears.
- **Battles that take their time.** Two even squads grind at each other for the best part of a minute, and a full battle runs a minute or two, so there's time to see a fight turning and send help.
- **Archers with a quiver.** Eight arrows a man: deadly against troops marching at them, but they run dry, and then they're poor fighters. Arrows aimed into a melee with your own men mostly go wide.
- **Morale and routing.** A squad that takes heavy losses breaks and runs for its own edge. Men who escape survive the battle.
- **Deploy your whole army.** Every squad in your warband is a card. Before the fight, place each one anywhere in your half of the field, with the enemy's line in plain view. Tap a placed squad to move it, or tap *Auto-place* for a battle line (swords and spears in front, bows behind, horse on the wings). Time stands still until you press *Fight*.
- **Two orders: Advance and Hold.** Squads hold their ground until ordered. *Advance* sends them at the enemy at marching pace, each going for the enemy it fights best, then charging when close. *Hold* stops them where they stand to fight whoever comes. The buttons command the whole army, or one squad once you select it (tap its card, its standard or its men); with a squad selected you can also tap an enemy squad to attack it (crossed swords and a pulsing ring mark every squad you've sent someone after, and the attackers stay on it until it breaks), or open ground to march there. Squads on the march go around enemy squads in their way instead of through them.
- **Draw a line.** Drag across the field and the whole army, or the squad you've picked, forms up along the line: each squad takes its own stretch in the order they already stand, so their paths don't cross, and faces away from the line toward the enemy. While you drag, the ground each squad will cover is outlined. A squad on its own widens or deepens its ranks to fill the line (never under two ranks). Before the battle, a line places the whole army at once.
- **Hold fire.** Archers can keep their arrows: they loose none, and draw blades if the enemy reaches them. A volley ends the hold.
- **Braced lines.** A squad that has stood its ground on *Hold* for two seconds is braced: in the fight that reaches it, it hits a third harder and takes a quarter less from the front while the lines are locked (up to six seconds). Letting the enemy come is often the right call; *Advance* gives up the brace.
- **Lines that hold together (switched off).** Squads now fight by taking sides of an enemy (below), and this is off for now; the code stays behind `formation_melee` in `battle_sim.gd`. Switched on: when two lines of foot meet, each keeps its ranks: it turns slowly to face the squad it fights, closes to a front where the two lines meet and fights across it, every man staying near his place in the line, and when a front-rank man falls the man behind steps up. A squad on *Hold* meets the enemy where it stands, and squads side by side shuffle apart rather than merge, and where a longer line overlaps a shorter one its end men fold in on the flank. Horsemen, bowmen (light troops, even once their quivers are empty), men floundering in a ford, and anyone fighting them, still fight as a loose melee, and so do the walls of a siege.
- **Squads take a side, not a scrum.** A squad going for an enemy takes a free side of it: two foot squads to an enemy, the first at its front and the next on a flank, while horse ride in on any side still open, preferring a flank or the rear. A third foot squad goes for another enemy, or, with none to spare, waits behind a friend's line as a reserve, stepping in when the enemy reaches it. A squad heading for a flank goes round the corner and joins the fight there, not on its way past the front, and the side it chose stays put on the ground, so an enemy that turns doesn't lead it in circles. An order to attack a particular enemy goes in whatever; archers shooting, ambushes and siege assaults go as before.
- **Flanks and rear.** A squad locked in one fight can't turn to meet a second enemy. Blows from beside or behind it land harder and shake its nerve, and horse that take spearmen from behind land their charge. Pin them with foot, then send in the horse.
- **One ability per squad.** Infantry raise a *Shield wall* (arrows and blows from the front barely land, but they move at half pace). Spearmen form a *Schiltron* (a ring of spears with no flank or rear, and no charge lands). Archers loose a *Volley* (every bow at once, harder, and it shakes the target's nerve). Cavalry *Wheel* (break off, ride clear, charge the same enemy again). Each has a cooldown.
- **Win by breaking their army.** The battle ends when one side has no squad left standing. After five minutes, the side with more of its army still fighting wins.
- **Enemy commanders with a temperament**, which your scouts report while you deploy. *Reckless* comes straight at you. *Patient* holds a braced line, takes your charge, then sends in the rest. *Hammer* pins you with foot and holds its horse until the lines meet, then rides for your archers and flanks. All of them use their squads' abilities, and all but the reckless use the ground: they shift their whole line onto the best footing in reach (the crest of a rise, the edge of a wood, the near bank of a ford), put their archers on the heights, and keep that ground for a while rather than wade a river or leave a ridge to come at you. A chief's bodyguard stands behind his line and rides out only to finish a fight or meet a threat.
- **A battle you can read on a phone.** The camera follows the fighting. Every squad carries a standard showing its type, morale and men, swallowtails for you and square flags for them; a squad's standard topples when it breaks. Select a squad and the enemy standards show which ones it beats and which beat it. A tray of squad cards along the bottom shows every squad's morale and whether its ability is ready. A strength bar replaces the clock. Pause any time (orders still work while paused), play at double speed, and the battle pauses itself if you leave the app.
- **Pixel-art troops, dressed by culture and by training.** Original top-down sprites for each type in both team colors, different for each people: Vardish footmen with kite shields and steel helms, kettle-hatted spearmen, hooded bowmen and horsemen in steel caps; Kharzani askari in white turbans with round shields and scimitars, spearmen, recurve bowmen and faris on grey horses; Ryumon samurai in black lacquer with crested helmets and sashimono banners at their backs, ashigaru under wide straw hats, long-bowed yumi archers and riders on dark horses. Each of the 24 trained branches wears its own kit, on the field and in its portrait: men-at-arms in plate with great helms, sergeants with maces and plumes, pikemen, halberdiers with poleaxes, crossbowmen with pavises on their backs, longbowmen, barded knights; javelin askari, gilded guard askari, spear guard in mail, shieldless levy spears, veiled desert archers, horse archers and faris lancers with pennons; hatamoto under horned crests, ronin with no banner, yari ashigaru and ashigaru archers under black lacquered hats, naginata, yumi samurai with a katana at the hip, mounted samurai with the horo billowing behind them, and mounted archers. The upgrade choice shows both. A squad you hire abroad wears its own culture's gear. Two walk frames and a strike pose each, a white flash when hit, shadows, fallen bodies, trampled and bloodied ground where they fell, and arrows that arc in flight and stick in the turf where they miss. Charges shake the screen and land with a jolt.
- **Fields by land.** A battle is fought on the ground of the realm it's in: Vardish meadow, Kharzani steppe with pebbles and scrub, Ryumon moss with fallen petals, with rocks, bushes and patches of lighter and darker ground.
- **A battlefield that shows pressure.** A squad close to breaking is marked wavering before it runs, its standard trembling; a squad that halts settles into its ground; arrows at the edge of the field point to your selected company, or to any of yours fighting out of sight. Big moments cut through the noise of routine fighting, charges throw up spray, leaf litter, sand or dust by the ground, and an optional vibration answers accepted orders and decisive moments.
- **Siege battles.** A wall crosses the field with a gate in it. Men on foot at the gate batter it down (horse at half the pace; horses can't climb the ladders). Rout the garrison to win; a gate still standing when time runs out means the walls held. Defend your own walls the same way from the other side.
- **A wall with a top and a hole.** While the gate holds it's a door: nobody fights through it, and the garrison never steps outside its own wall. The fighting happens where a wall is weak. The garrison has posts: bows on the wall walk beside each ladder (the battlements there halve arrows; the courtyard behind gets no cover), a company of swords on the gate tower dropping stones on the men at the ram (whose roof takes most of it), and the rest of its foot in a crescent behind the gate where the breach will come. When men go up a ladder, the nearest free company on foot climbs to the ladder's head; a held head slows the climb, and the man topping the ladder fights one-handed. When the gate breaks, the garrison's idle foot falls back to the crescent and holds the breach, and it sallies only once the fight turns its way (or nobody has come for it in a while). The attackers spread out too: *Storm* (and an enemy storming your walls) sends the companies furthest out on each side up a ladder each when there are three or more on foot, the rest to the ram, and the bows stand off at bow range to shoot the walk rather than walk up to the wall; once their quivers are empty they wait for the gate. Holding your own walls, *Auto-place* puts your companies at their posts (drawn while you deploy: the gate tower, the ladder heads, the crescent), *Man the walls* sends everyone back to them, *Hold breach* forms the crescent once the gate is down, and *Advance all* becomes *Sally*. In seeded AI battles on a castle, an even assault took it in none of eight (the garrison lost about a quarter of its men), half again the garrison won 20 of 32 and lost some 84 men to the garrison's 58 on average, and twice the garrison won all eight. While you deploy, the ground the wall's bows reach is shaded, along with the cover under the ram's roof and the two ladders. *Storm* shows its plan first (dashed routes from each company to a ladder or the ram, and the battle waits); tap a card to swap a company between ladder and ram, then *Storm!* to go. Each company's card says what it's doing (climbing, on the walls, at the ram, inside, and for a garrison: stones on the tower, holding a ladder, at the breach), the gate has its own bar over the gatehouse, the breach gets a banner, and the result tells the siege's story: who held the castle after, the blows on the gate, the men who went over the ladders, the stones dropped and the men thrown off the ladders, and when each turn came.
- **Captains who can die.** Every squad is led by a named captain ("Brann's Shields"), a tougher man at the heart of the formation marked by a gold ring, with a temperament (Vengeful, Steadfast, Fragile) and sometimes a quirk (Lucky, Mentor, Bold). The squad holds its nerve better under them and reels when they go down.
- **The death roll.** A fallen captain rolls a d100 against a death chance of 45%, less 15 each if their own squad still has half its men, another squad is close enough to drag them clear, or they're Lucky. Over the chance: wounded, and they sit out the next two battles. At or under: dead. The battle all but stops while the die rolls on screen against the odds, with each modifier shown, then their fate and last words; the dead go on the memorial in camp while a veteran takes over the company.
- **Bonds.** Captains who fight side by side build a bond, rank C, B then A. Bonded pairs fighting near each other regain nerve and, from B, hit harder. When one falls, the other reacts by temperament: fury, a steadied line, or a squad that starts to break. The Warband's Bonds tab shows talks waiting at the fire first (its tab, the camp and the map's Warband button count them), then bonds closest to their next rank, pairs still growing toward C, and the remembered. A talk at the fire puts the two captains face to face by the campfire at night: what the company remembers of their battles, then a short exchange in their own words, with a tab for each rank reached to read it again.
- **A persistent warband** with its bonds and the memorial of the fallen, on the Warband screen from the map.

### Sound and feel

- **Music for every place**, composed for the game (`tools/music.py`, `tools/orchestral.py`). Home opens on The Glass Sea, played by a recorded orchestra: harp alone, then the horn's tune, then the violins take it up over the whole orchestra before it settles; once you have a campaign, Home plays your own realm's tune by the campfire instead. On the map, the music of the realm you're riding through, crossfading as you cross a border: lute and recorder over a hurdy-gurdy drone in Vardmark, oud and ney over a frame drum's rhythm in Kharzan, koto and shakuhachi with taiko on the Ryumon isles.
- **Battle music in layers**: while you deploy, a drone, a heartbeat drum and a call to arms in the enemy's style (Vardmark's horns, Kharzan's ney, Ryumon's shakuhachi and taiko; sieges have their own, heavier). Press Fight and the full music starts: drums and a string ostinato written to carry on a phone speaker, the tune swelling in as more of the field is fighting, bright trumpets when the fight goes your way and dark tremolo when your lines waver. Each piece runs a minute, in four sections with a breakdown before the climax. The victory or defeat call waits for the beat, then a quiet piece plays under the results.
- **Battle effects**: sparks fly where blows land, with a spatter now and then, and a warm glow along the front shows where the lines meet. Dust trails every squad on the move, thicker behind horses and coloured by the ground, and charging riders stream speed lines. Arrows trail streaks and the ground darkens where a volley will land. Men stagger and fall rather than vanish, leaving shield, weapon and a dark patch; a broken squad strews its gear and leaves its standard in the mud. Standards ripple in the wind; a wavering squad's men glance back over their shoulders; when their captain falls, time slows and the field darkens round him; and when the battle is won, the winners cheer under glowing standards before the result comes up. Reduced motion stills the ripple, glances, cheer and speed lines.
- **Men who fight, not flash.** Each man draws back before a blow, lunges into it on the strike frame and eases back to his place; a hit knocks him back and turns him; a charge shoves the men it strikes back the way the horses came, the rank behind half as far, and men duck under a volley coming down. The dying stagger before they fall, with dust where they land. A braced line, a shield wall or a schiltron crouches into the blow; men waiting breathe on their own beat; routers face the edge they run for and lean into it. Where the lines meet, men are drawn back to front across squads. All of it is drawn only: the same seeded battle ends the same with the screen drawing it or not (`tests/battle_feel_tests.gd`).
- **Impact, and words with a weight.** A charge on or by your men stops the battle for longer the more men it strikes (charges landing together share one stop), knocks the view the way it went and punches in toward it. Callouts come in three tiers: a moment (your squad breaking, a captain cut down, the chief or a lord falling, the gate) stamps onto the field on a plate, one at a time, while the rest wait; a squad's word (CHARGE, BRACED, FLANK, a broken enemy) is a pill over its standard, one per squad and never twice in three seconds; a state (wavering, out of arrows) is a badge on the standard and the card, with no words.
- **The hour of the fight.** A battle is fought at the hour the world's clock says: dawn is pale and warm with mist on the far edge and long shadows east, dusk orange with long shadows west, night blue with a vignette and light pooled round the standards. One wind per battle carries the dust.
- **A start and an end.** *Fight* puts up a title card (the place, the ground and the hour, the odds and the scouts' word on their commander) while the view pulls out from your line; the battle waits for it, and a tap clears it. When it's decided, VICTORY stamps on the field under a spotlight on the last squad to break, or the colour drains from the field under DEFEAT, before the result, which now opens on a picture of the field. The tray feels the fight: a card's edge flashes red when its squad is charged or flanked, a broken squad's card cracks and shakes, morale bars and the strength bar ease down with a pale bite for what was just lost, and an ability coming ready pulses once with a click. Pausing greys the field. The death roll's plate drops in, its die tumbles and bounces as it lands, the odds fill before the number, and FALLEN stamps down. *Screen shake: off, light or full* in Settings; reduced motion keeps every word and state and stills every offset, stamp, zoom and shake.
- **Battle sounds** that play where they happen, from the left or right of the screen, duller and quieter when you're zoomed out. Blows ring out where the lines meet, matched to who's fighting (sword on sword, a thunk on a shield, spear shafts knocking, axes and maces crashing), each in a dozen takes that never repeat back to back, over the din of the melee that swells and fades with the fighting. Arrows, bolts and javelins are heard going up and coming down. Men roar as they charge, horsemen thunder into the line, shields lock with a shout when spears brace, a squad that breaks scatters shouting while the other side cheers, a captain falls with a gasp from the ranks. Sieges have the ram's blows on the gate, ladders clattering on stone and the gate giving way. Each realm sounds its own calls to start, charge and retreat (Vardmark's horns and trumpets, Kharzan's shawm and drums, Ryumon's conch and taiko), and its distant horn when more of them come. Built from recorded metal and wood (public domain) with synthesized bodies and crowds, every sound carries on a phone speaker, and a limiter keeps the loudest moments clean. Each battle sound plays at its own target level over the battle music (which sits lower than music elsewhere): routine blows and arrows a few dB over the drums, charges, horns and a captain's fall well over them, the din of the melee under them. Every take is trimmed from its measured level (`tools/sfx_levels.py` writes `scripts/data/sfx_levels.gd`), so no blow is lost to a quiet recording.
- **Other sounds**: the snap and twang of crossbows, the whoosh of thrown javelins, horse archers loosing on the move, a fanfare when a chief's guard breaks or he falls, the river running through a ford and birds in a wood under the battle music, the rattle of the death roll's die and a bell when it lands on death; on the map, open-road wind, woods, water, night and settlement-edge ambience crossfade under foot or cavalry movement, with an alarm horn when bandits block the road and coins when you buy or hire.
- **Feel**: screens fade into one another, every button clicks and gives under your finger, map sheets and the battle result ease into view, and the title screen drifts over the Glass Sea at dawn. Music and sound can each be switched off on the dedicated Settings page, alongside text size, reduced motion, screen shake and vibration; the choices are kept on the device.

## Run it

1. Download **Godot 4.7** (the standard build, not .NET) from [godotengine.org](https://godotengine.org/download).
2. Open Godot, choose **Import**, and select `project.godot` in this folder.
3. Press **Play** (F5). The window is phone-shaped; a mouse click works as a tap. You start at the title screen: New campaign, then choose your people.

## Put it on your iPhone

You need a Mac with Xcode and an Apple ID (a free one works for your own phone).

1. In Godot: **Editor → Manage Export Templates → Download and Install**.
2. **Project → Export → Add… → iOS.** Fill in **App Store Team ID** (your team ID from Xcode or developer.apple.com) and **Bundle Identifier** (for example `com.yourname.bannerfall`).
3. **Export Project** to a folder. Godot writes an Xcode project.
4. Open the `.xcodeproj` in Xcode, plug in your iPhone, pick it as the run destination, and press **Run**.

The project uses the Compatibility renderer, which also runs in the iOS Simulator. Godot's guide: [Exporting for iOS](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_ios.html).

## Tests

For menu navigation, tutorial opt-in and replay, first-bond save behavior, resource controls and desertion warnings, run `godot --headless --path . --script res://tests/menu_tests.gd`. It uses a temporary preferences file and does not save its test campaign.

From the project folder:

```
godot --headless --script res://tests/run_tests.gd
```

On a Mac, `godot` is the binary inside the app: `/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot`.

To smoke-test the whole loop, run the game with `-- --autoplay`: it starts a Vardish campaign, rides at the nearest bandits, lets the computer fight both sides of the battle, returns to the map, sends the men in against the next band, then enters the next settlement it reaches, buys food, hires and reinforces, and quits.

It checks the deployment rules (no placing in the enemy half, squads dropped on each other get nudged apart, Auto-place fits everyone, the clock waits for *Fight*), that *Hold* keeps a squad in place and *Advance* sends it forward, that archers run out of arrows, lines (squads share a drawn line in the order they stand, without overlaps, centered where it was drawn; they march there, hold and face the enemy; one squad alone widens or deepens to fill it; before the battle a line places everyone where the preview showed, and stays in your half), hold fire (archers keep every arrow and fight with blades; a volley ends the hold), the tactics (only a pinned squad can be flanked; horse in the rear of a pinned squad break it sooner and lose fewer riders; a braced line wins the even fight it receives; horse sent at archers ride around the spears in front of them), each ability, that each kind of enemy commander behaves as its scouts say, the cultures (each warband's captains and companies come from its own culture, and it starts at its capital), the world (every settlement reaches every other by road, settlements and roads lie on land and sea lanes cross water, travel takes exactly the time the map says, the clock stands still when you do, a band on the road stops you and the clock, a beaten band comes back a day later in its own realm, a saved world loads back exactly and carries on exactly as the original, a castle that changes hands takes its villages along and a realm that loses its capital rules from what it has left, owners and wars are saved, a save from before they could change still loads, sixty days of riding never puts more than two bands in one realm, and battle results reach the map), the economy (a day costs a day's wages and a ration a man; hungry or unpaid men desert in proportion to the shortfall; hiring brings a full squad of the offering place's culture, only where you stand and only under the squad cap, which renown raises; offers come back after three days; reinforcing costs silver and waters down experience; training raises level and wages; spoils and renown are paid; a band that beats you keeps only its survivors), auto-resolve (the side it reckons stronger wins the full battle at least 85% of the time over sixteen random battles, with about the same losses; every man is accounted for; men sent in without you lose more; captains can fall), six scripted 30-day campaigns that settle every fight unled (`Plans.campaign`: every warband lasts the month without dropping below 50 men, rarely deserts or goes broke, makes a name, and most grow), that each counter wins its duel at least 80% of the time, that mirror matches are close to even, that full AI-vs-AI battles finish with an army breaking and last about a minute, and that every man is accounted for in the battle result. It takes a few minutes and exits with code 1 if anything fails.

Add `-- --balance` to also fight three scripted players against every kind of commander, twelve battles each (`tests/plans.gd`): one who presses *Advance all* and nothing else, one who holds all battle, and a tactician who receives the charge braced, uses volleys and shield walls, and sends the horse into flanks. It checks that skill pays: *Advance all* wins about half the time at most, the tactician at least 70%. It also checks the Phase 3 gates: mirrored maps of every kind are fair both ways round; attacking a band that holds a ridge, a ford or a wood's edge with 15% more men wins at least 15 points less often than on open ground; each culture's trained army is a match for each other's; the tactician wins each objective at least 60% of the time, and clearly more often than *Advance all*; and neither branch of a role outclasses the other. It takes the best part of an hour.
